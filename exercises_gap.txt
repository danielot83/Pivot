How to use Create exercise
This page always shows a ready-to-fill exercise — the "Number" field at the top is assigned automatically the moment you land here, and it never changes no matter which of the 3 buttons below you use. No need to click anything first. The small text next to "Number" shows how many exercises your club already has, so the number has some context — it only counts your own club's exercises, not ones shared by other clubs.

Court diagram section
— 5 diagrams, each independent — they're not steps you have to fill in order. Each one has its own "This diagram's court" dropdown (half court / vertical / full court) — they start half/half/half/vertical/full by default, but pick whatever you need for each, including all 5 the same, if that's clearer for what you're drawing. Pick a tool, click the court to place a player/defender/cone/etc, or click-drag to draw a movement, pass, screen, or zone.
Color
picker lets you distinguish sequences (e.g. move 1 in black, move 2 in red).
Copy to next diagram
duplicates everything into the next diagram — handy when you ARE drawing a sequence: draw diagram 1, copy it forward, then just move a couple of things for diagram 2, instead of starting from scratch each time. Any movement/dribble/pass/shot arrow is applied first (the player or ball lands where the arrow pointed) and doesn't carry over itself, so diagram 2 starts from the result, ready for its own arrows.
🗑 Delete element
— pick "Move/Select", click the player/arrow/zone/etc. you want gone, then this button (or Del/Backspace on a keyboard) removes just that one.
Export PDF
downloads the exercise sheet straight away: your club's logo and name, number, name, only the categories/material you actually picked, objectives, difficulty stars, description, variants, and all 5 diagrams — same one-page sheet as the desktop app.

Fill in from a photo — what it actually does
This is NOT AI — it's plain text recognition (OCR), running on your own device. No account, no setup, no internet service reading your exercises.
reads the letters it can see in the photo and drops that text into "Description" — saves you typing it out by hand.
good light, the sheet held flat and filling the frame, and fairly neat handwriting (or printed text) all make a real difference. Very messy handwriting may come out garbled — that's the technology's limit, not a bug.
