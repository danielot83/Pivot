2.Who's responsible for what

PlayPivot is used by clubs all over the world — and data protection law isn't the same everywhere. PlayPivot doesn't decide which law applies to you: the laws of your own country do, whether that's an EU country, Switzerland, Bangladesh, or anywhere else. This document can't list every country's rules, so instead here's the chain of responsibility that holds regardless of where you are:

Player / family  →  Club  →  PlayPivot  →  Infomaniak (hosting)

  • Your club is the data controller for the sporting data it enters — rosters, assessments, attendance. Your club decides which players to register, what to record about them, and why. That's true whether the data lives in PlayPivot, a spreadsheet, or a paper folder — the club, through its admin and coaches, is responsible for having a valid reason (and where required by the law that applies to you, valid consent) to hold that data in the first place.
  • PlayPivot is the data processor for that same sporting data — we act on your club's instructions, we don't sell it, and we don't use it for advertising or our own purposes.
  • PlayPivot is itself the controller for a narrower set of data: your own login/account details, security logs, and anything related to billing or subscriptions — data PlayPivot needs to run the platform and keep it secure, independent of any single club's instructions.
  • Infomaniak, our hosting provider, is PlayPivot's sub-processor. They host the infrastructure PlayPivot runs on, under their own data-processing terms with PlayPivot. If PlayPivot ever adds another vendor that touches personal data (email delivery, analytics, AI features), it gets added here too — see section 6a.

Practically: if you're a club admin or coach adding a player, you're confirming you already have the right to hold that player's information under the law that applies where you are — through the club's own registration process, a parent/guardian's consent, or however that normally works in your country or federation. PlayPivot doesn't verify this for you, the same way a spreadsheet or a paper folder never did either.

Two examples of what "the law that applies to you" can look like, since they come up often for clubs using PlayPivot: the EU's GDPR generally requires parental consent for a child's data below age 16 (countries can lower this to 13); Switzerland's data protection act treats a child's own consent as valid only once they have "capacity of judgement" (usually around 13), with a parent or guardian deciding below that. These are examples, not a summary of every country's law — check what applies to you specifically if you're unsure.

3.Acceptable use — what's allowed, what isn't

PlayPivot is built for one purpose: running a basketball team or club. That means:

Allowed

  • Adding data about players you or your club actually coach or manage.
  • Sharing your own exercises, plays, and content with your team, your club association, or the wider PlayPivot community, at whatever circle you choose.
  • Using another club or coach's shared content (exercises, plays) within PlayPivot, the way it's meant to be used — for your own coaching.

Not allowed

  • Adding data about someone you don't have the right to hold data about (see section 2).
  • Copying another club's shared content and republishing it outside PlayPivot as if it were your own.
  • Using PlayPivot to store or share anything unrelated to coaching a team — this isn't general-purpose file storage.
  • Trying to access another club's private data, or another account's credentials, without authorization.

We can suspend or remove an account that breaks these terms, particularly anything touching a minor's data inappropriately.

4.What PlayPivot stores, and what it doesn't

We try to collect the minimum needed to run a team, not everything that's technically possible to collect. A few concrete choices:

  • No player photos. PlayPivot doesn't store or upload pictures of players.
  • No license documents, no scanned IDs, no national identification numbers (like Switzerland's AVS number) in Roster. A license number (just the reference number, nothing behind it) and a simple "Ready" checkbox are as far as that goes.
  • Everything else in Roster (name, birth year, category, gender) is there because a club needs it to run a team — and every field is optional except a first and last name.
  • Occasional product emails. If you choose to opt in, we may email you a couple of times a year about major new PlayPivot features — never more often, never sold or shared with anyone else, and you can opt out again at any time.

5.Data about minors specifically

Most players in a youth basketball club are minors, so this matters more here than in most software. A few things worth knowing:

  • Player assessment — technique, game IQ, personality, and attitude scores — counts as evaluative data about a minor. Get comfortable with your club's own policy on this before filling one in; it's not something to enter casually.
  • Adding a minor's data may legally require a parent or guardian's consent before you enter it, depending on where your club and its players are based — see the examples in section 2. This is the club's responsibility to obtain, not something PlayPivot checks for you.
  • A parent or guardian can ask their club to show them, correct, or delete everything PlayPivot holds about their child — see section 7.

6.Who can see what

Nothing in PlayPivot is shared more widely than you choose. Almost everything you create has its own "Who can see it?" setting:

  • 🔒 Only me — private, not even your own teammates see it.
  • 👥 My team — everyone active in your club (the default for most things).
  • 🤝 My club association — a small group of clubs who agreed to share, needs approval.
  • 🌐 PlayPivot Community — every club on PlayPivot can see it (view only).

Two things this circle system does not apply to, on purpose: Player assessment never gets shared in bulk with a "player" account, even if a coach sets it to "My team" — a linked player can only ever see their own. And a player account never sees anything just from being on the team — a coach has to explicitly choose to share it with them (for example, turning on "Show to players" on a specific play).

7.Who else touches your data

PlayPivot doesn't run its own servers — like almost every online service, it runs on infrastructure operated by someone else, under a contract that limits what they can do with what passes through it. Right now, that's:

  • Infomaniak — hosting and infrastructure. Their published documentation states their data centers are located and operated in Switzerland.

If PlayPivot ever adds a vendor that can access personal data — email delivery, analytics, an AI feature — it gets added to this list before it goes live, not after. We don't quietly add data-touching vendors.

Analytics. PlayPivot uses Matomo to understand overall usage (which pages get visited, roughly how often) — self-hosted on our own Infomaniak infrastructure, not sent to a third-party analytics company. It's configured cookieless and anonymizes visitor IP addresses, so it doesn't build a profile of you individually or track you across other websites.

8.Access, correction, and deletion

A parent, guardian, or player old enough to act for themselves can ask their club (or PlayPivot directly — see Contact) for:

  • A copy of everything held about that player.
  • A correction to anything inaccurate.
  • Deletion of that player's data. A club admin can remove a player from Roster at any time, which removes their record and everything tied to it (assessments, attendance, match stats).

For your own account: Settings → "Delete my account & data" lets you leave any club instantly (its data stays intact for other members), or delete a club entirely if you're its only admin — that removes everything in it immediately. We keep the right to delete, on request, anything you created that was never shared with anyone else (private notes, drafts) — content you shared with your club (a play, an exercise marked "My team" or wider) stays with the club, since other people rely on it too. Deleting the login itself (your email/password) needs a person on our side to complete, since that's not something the app can do on its own for security reasons — the same page sends that request directly.

We aim to act on these within a reasonable time, and always within what local law requires (for example, one month under GDPR).

9.Security

Data is stored with row-level access rules, meaning the database itself — not just the app's interface — enforces who can read or write what, based on their role in the club. Passwords are never visible to us; authentication is handled by our database provider's standard login system.

If something goes wrong, here's who does what:

  • A security incident inside PlayPivot's own systems — PlayPivot investigates, contains it, and notifies affected clubs as required by law.
  • An incident at a hosting/infrastructure provider (section 7) — PlayPivot coordinates with that provider and passes on what clubs need to know.
  • A correction or export request from a parent — the club handles it, with PlayPivot providing the technical means to do so.
